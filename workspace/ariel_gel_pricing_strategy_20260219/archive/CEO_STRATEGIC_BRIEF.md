## 2. Top 3 Strategic Findings

### Finding 1: We raised our price, Attack cut theirs, and we lost 1.78 million buyers

**What happened:** In May 2025, Ariel Gel raised the regular bottle (本体通常) price by +29%. At the same time, Attack 抗菌EX cut the same size by -22%. The shelf price gap more than doubled overnight. Since then, 1.78 million Ariel Gel shoppers have stopped purchasing. 410,000 of them walked straight to Attack — buying the exact same pack size from the competitor.

**Why it matters:** ¥2.58 billion in annual revenue is at risk. The regular bottle has the worst lapse rate of any Ariel size at 54.9% — more than half of all buyers left. This is not a slow erosion; it is a structural break caused by a specific pricing decision.

**What to do:** Bring the regular bottle everyday shelf price below ¥350 immediately. Continue ¥200–250 trial promotions at the top 3 retailers with the widest Attack price gap. But critically — pair every regular bottle promotion with a refill upgrade offer (詰替超特大 at ≤¥450) so trial buyers have a path into Ariel's loyalty engine. Customer Development Lead identifies target retailers and executes within 30 days.

**¥ impact:** Converting 10% of lapsed buyers into refill purchasers = ~40,000 buyers recovered → ¥25M–67M/year in direct revenue, plus multi-year compounding through refill loyalty.

---

### Finding 2: When our buyers leave, they buy the exact same size from Attack — every size is a direct head-to-head battle

**What happened:** Shopper switching is not random. 95% of lapsed Mega Jumbo buyers went to Attack Mega Jumbo. 73% of lapsed Super Ultra refill buyers went to Attack Super Ultra refill. 45% of lapsed 詰替超特大 buyers went to Attack 詰替超特大. The competitive fight is size-by-size, not brand-by-brand.

**Why it matters:** A ¥50 price gap on Mega Jumbo costs us Mega Jumbo buyers — not regular bottle buyers. Brand-level pricing strategy is the wrong unit of management. Every size needs its own competitive pricing rule.

**What to do:** The Customer Development team must pull a weekly price-gap report for the 3 highest-risk sizes (Mega Jumbo, Super Ultra refill, Regular Bottle) at the top 5 retailers. If Attack's shelf price drops more than ¥50 below Ariel on any of these sizes, a pricing review meeting fires within 48 hours — not at the next monthly cycle.

**¥ impact:** Retaining 5% of the 410,000 size-matched defectors = ~20,500 buyers/year → ¥60M–80M/year in revenue retained.

---

### Finding 3: Ariel's product creates fierce loyalty on refill sizes — but we are pricing ourselves out of the first purchase

**What happened:** On every refill size, Ariel's repeat rate beats Attack by 12–17 percentage points. 92% of Mega Jumbo buyers who come back buy the same pack again. The product wins — when shoppers get through the door. But lapsed Mega Jumbo buyers were paying ¥980 at their last purchase. Loyal buyers were paying ¥949. A ¥31 difference is the line between keeping a customer and losing them.

**Why it matters:** A loyal Mega Jumbo buyer purchases roughly 4 times per year at ¥949 = ¥3,796/year. Over 3 years, that is ¥11,400 in lifetime value. Letting first-purchase prices creep above ¥950 destroys ¥11,400 in future value to capture a one-time ¥31 premium. The math is not close.

**What to do:** Set hard price ceilings: Mega Jumbo first-purchase ≤ ¥950. Ultra Jumbo first-purchase ≤ ¥800. Enforce at all 9 retailers. No exceptions. Category Revenue Management owns compliance; confirmed at all retailers by March 15.

**¥ impact:** 30,000 new loyal Mega Jumbo shoppers from a 20% trial increase → ~¥342M in 3-year lifetime value.

## 4. Competitive Window Analysis

### How long before Attack's advantage becomes entrenched?

Attack is accumulating ~85,000 additional loyal buyers over Ariel every month. Loyalty in this category is structural — once a shopper establishes a refill habit with Attack, they purchase 3–4 times per year for multiple years. Each month of delay does not create a one-month problem; it creates a multi-year compounding disadvantage.

Based on current trajectories:

- **By May 2026** (3 months): Attack gains ~252,000 additional loyal buyers, ~¥239M in annualized revenue permanently transferred
- **By August 2026** (6 months): ~504,000 buyers, ~¥478M annualized
- **By February 2027** (12 months): ~1,020,000 buyers, ~¥960M annualized — approaching ¥1 billion in annual revenue structurally shifted to the competitor

The entrenchment horizon is approximately 6–9 months. After that, Attack's refill buyer base will be so large that even aggressive Ariel promotions will struggle to break established repurchase habits.

### Cost of 90-day delay vs cost of action

| Scenario                                                            | Buyers lost                          | ¥ Revenue impact                                                    | Nature of loss                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **90-day delay**                                                    | ~252,000 additional buyers to Attack | ~¥239M annualized revenue                                           | **Permanent** — these become Attack loyalists                       |
| **Cost of action** (price ceiling enforcement + promo reallocation) | Near-zero incremental budget         | Budget-neutral (reallocation from Super Ultra refill to Mega Jumbo) | **Recoverable** — trial investments compound through refill loyalty |

The cost of action is near-zero beyond execution effort. The cost of waiting is ¥239M every 90 days, compounding.

### Where Ariel still has a structural advantage

1. **Product quality creates loyalty.** On every refill size, Ariel's repeat rate beats Attack by 12–17 percentage points. Once a shopper establishes an Ariel refill habit, they stay. The product is not the problem — the entry price is.

2. **Regular bottle shopper base.** 本体通常 is the only size where Ariel has more shoppers than Attack (1,008,908 vs 905,147). The ¥198 trial strategy worked for acquisition. The pipeline of potential refill converts already exists.

3. **Mega Jumbo loyalty fortress.** 92% same-pack repeat. Price promotions convert in 94/100 store-weeks. This size is Ariel's competitive moat — if buyers reach it, they stay.

4. **~890,000 category-exit shoppers are not locked into Attack.** Half of all lapsed Ariel shoppers did not go to Attack — they stopped buying liquid detergent. This is a win-back pool that requires a separate reactivation campaign, but the key point is: these shoppers are not lost to the competitor.

These structural advantages are real but depreciating. Each month of price-gap disadvantage erodes the trial pipeline and delays entry into the loyalty engine where Ariel's product advantage activates.

1. **¥ values on every claim.** No finding without a revenue number. "Shoppers are lapsing" is not acceptable — "406,262 shoppers lapsed from regular bottle, putting ~¥310M/year at risk" is the minimum standard.

5. **Size-level strategy, not brand-level.** The deliverable must make clear that each pack size competes directly against its Attack equivalent. Brand-level pricing is the wrong lens.

## CEO Verdict

The data is unambiguous. Ariel Gel's May 2025 price increase — executed simultaneously with Attack's price cut — opened a shelf price gap that is bleeding 85,000 loyal buyers per month. The product quality advantage (12–17pp higher repeat rates than Attack on every refill size) is being negated by pricing that prevents shoppers from reaching the sizes where that advantage activates.

**Three things must happen:**

1. **Fix the front door** — regular bottle shelf price ≤¥350, paired with refill upgrade offers at ≤¥450
2. **Protect the fortress** — Mega Jumbo ≤¥950, Ultra Jumbo ≤¥800, enforce at all 9 retailers
3. **Reallocate budget to where it works** — move Super Ultra refill price-off spend to Mega Jumbo, where price drives trial in 94% of store-weeks

These are not aspirational recommendations. They are survival actions. The cost of inaction is ¥239M every 90 days, permanently transferred to Attack.
