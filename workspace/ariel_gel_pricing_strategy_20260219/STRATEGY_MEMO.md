# 本体通常 ¥198 Trial Drive Successfully Grew Ariel Gel Shoppers in 2025 — But High Lapse from Price-Induced Trial Means Refill Retention Is Now the Critical Battleground

**Prepared for:** Brand Management & Marketing Leadership
**Period:** January 2025 – January 2026 | 9 National Retailers | Loyalty Cardholders
**Date:** February 19, 2026

---

## Executive Summary

- **Ariel raised its regular bottle price +29% in May 2025 while Attack cut theirs -22%.** That single set of decisions more than doubled the visible shelf gap at the most-checked product on the shelf, and it is the primary origin of the buyer loss documented in this report.
- Ariel Gel is a **smaller brand than Attack 抗菌EX** — this context matters when interpreting new-buyer ratios. Despite the scale gap, the **本体通常 ¥198 promotional price in 2025 successfully recruited trial shoppers and grew the number of Ariel Gel buyers year-on-year.** Attack still attracted approximately twice the raw number of first-time buyers (2.16M vs. 1.14M), but Ariel grew its shopper base — a meaningful achievement for a smaller brand.
- **The trial was successful in bringing shoppers in — but low-quality trial.** At ¥198, many buyers experienced the product at an unsustainable price point and did not return at regular prices. **1.78 million Ariel buyers stopped purchasing** — roughly 410,000 moved directly to Attack. Lapse from 本体通常 is the worst of all sizes at **54.9%**.
- Ariel's product quality is a proven advantage: on every refill size, Ariel's share of first-time buyers who return for a second purchase beats Attack by **12 to 17 percentage points** — the problem is not the product, it is the quality of the first-purchase occasion and the price at which trial shoppers re-encounter the brand.

---

## Urgency

At the current pace, Attack closes approximately **84,000 additional loyal buyer gap versus Ariel every month** (1.02M annual gap ÷ 12). Waiting one additional quarter means ceding an estimated **~252,000 buyers and ~¥239M in annual revenue** that Attack will compound into multi-year customer relationships. The cost of waiting 90 days is not 90 days of delay — it is permanent share loss.

## Key Insights

### Key Insight 1: The ¥198 本体通常 Trial Was Strategically Right — But It Created Low-Quality Trial. The Next Step Is Converting Those Buyers Into Refill Users.

- **What we found:** 本体通常 has the highest lapse rate of all Ariel sizes at **54.9%** (406,262 lapsed out of 740,575 buyers). The ¥198 promotional price successfully recruited trial shoppers and grew total Ariel Gel shopper count in 2025 — the strategy worked on acquisition. However, shoppers who lapsed averaged only **¥255** at their last purchase vs. ¥408 for those who stayed, confirming that low-price trial attracted shoppers who did not sustain at regular price.
- **Why it matters:** The ¥198 trial was a top-of-funnel success but created a retention problem. These shoppers exist in the loyalty database — they tried Ariel Gel and left. The right next action is not more ¥198 promotions; it is a pathway from 本体通常 trial to refill sizes where Ariel's retention advantage (12–17pp above Attack) kicks in.
- **What to do:** Continue targeted ¥200–250 本体通常 promotions at top retailers to maintain trial flow. Simultaneously, design a refill upgrade offer — e.g., a 詰替超特大 at ≤¥450 alongside 本体通常 purchase — to funnel trial buyers into the size where Ariel's quality retention advantage is strongest. Customer Development to identify the 3 retailers with the widest 本体通常 price gap vs. Attack for immediate action.
- **Expected impact:** Converting even 10% of the 406,262 lapsed 本体通常 buyers into 詰替超特大 repeat purchasers = ~40,000 buyers entering the refill retention engine → **¥25M–67M/year** in recovered revenue (estimate). Long-term compounding through refill loyalty is the larger prize.

### Key Insight 1b: 詰替超特大 Is the Critical Transition Size — Largest Volume After 詰替超ｳﾙﾄﾗｼﾞｬﾝﾎﾞ and the Direct Head-to-Head with Attack 抗菌EX Same Size

- **What we found:** 詰替超特大 has **1,108,637 buyers** (2nd largest size by shoppers for Ariel Gel) and a **44.6% lapse rate** (494,040 lapsed). Critically, among lapsed 詰替超特大 buyers who switched to Attack, **45% went to Attack 詰替超特大** — this is the clearest same-size competitive confrontation at intermission scale.
- **Why it matters:** This size sits at the strategic transition between trial (本体通常) and full-loyalty (詰替超ｳﾙﾄﾗｼﾞｬﾝﾎﾞ / 詰替ﾒｶﾞｼﾞｬﾝﾎﾞ). Attack 抗菌EX 詰替超特大 has 2,255,320 buyers vs. Ariel's 1,108,637 — Attack is more than double the size here. **Price gap at this size is of critical strategic importance** — a shopper who gets priced out of 詰替超特大 either downgrades back to 本体通常 or switches to Attack at the same size.
- **What to do:** Monitor and defend the price gap on 詰替超特大 vs. Attack 詰替超特大 weekly. Set a maximum acceptable price gap trigger of ¥50. At equivalent or near-equivalent price, Ariel's quality retention advantage should win — do not concede this size via pricing. Run targeted promotions to recruit 本体通常 trial buyers up to 詰替超特大 as the upgrade path.
- **Expected impact:** Closing the 2× shopper gap with Attack at 詰替超特大 over 2 years represents ~1.1M additional buyer-occasions per year at ~¥337 average = **~¥370M in annual revenue upside** (estimate, if share gap halves). Protecting existing 詰替超特大 buyers from further lapse is the immediate priority.

### Key Insight 2: Ariel's Refill Sizes Create Customers Who Almost Never Leave — but First-Purchase Price Must Stay Below a Ceiling

- **What we found:** 92% same-pack repurchase on Mega Jumbo; lapsed buyers paid ¥980 vs. loyal buyers' ¥949.
- **Why it matters:** Refill repeat rates beat Attack by 12–17pp on every size. A single loyal Mega Jumbo buyer is worth: **4 purchases/year × ¥949 = ¥3,796/year. Over 3 years: ~¥11,400 in lifetime value (estimate).** At 30,000 new loyal shoppers per year from the proposed trial price investment: **~¥342M in cumulative 3-year lifetime value (estimate: 30,000 × ¥11,400).** This is the return on holding the first-purchase price within the loyalty-generating ceiling — not a cost.
- **What to do:** Hard ceiling: Mega Jumbo ≤ ¥950, Ultra Jumbo ≤ ¥800 at first purchase.
- **Expected impact:** ~30,000 new loyal shoppers/year from Mega Jumbo alone, representing ~¥342M in 3-year lifetime value (estimate).

### Key Insight 3: When an Ariel Buyer Leaves, They Go Directly to the Equivalent Attack Size — Not to Another Brand

- **What we found:** 95% of Mega Jumbo leavers → Attack Mega Jumbo; 73% of Ultra Jumbo leavers → Attack Ultra Jumbo.
- **Why it matters:** Every size-level price decision has an immediate, direct competitive consequence.
- **What to do:** The Customer Development team pulls a **weekly price-gap report** for the 3 highest-risk sizes — Mega Jumbo, Ultra Jumbo, and Regular Bottle — at the top 5 retailers. If Attack's shelf price drops more than **¥50 below Ariel's price** on any of these sizes at any of these retailers, a **pricing review meeting is called within 48 hours**. The **Customer Development Lead** owns this meeting and the decision to respond.
- **Expected impact:** Retaining even 5% of size-switch defectors = 22,000+ shoppers/year.

### Key Insight 4: Price Promotions Work Powerfully on Some Sizes and Have Zero Effect on Others

- **What we found:** Mega Jumbo price cuts bring new buyers in 94/100 historical store-weeks. Super Ultra refill: zero measurable response to price changes.
- **Why it matters:** Price-off spending on the Super Ultra refill is entirely wasted for trial generation.
- **What to do:** The current Super Ultra refill price-off budget **[to be confirmed with Finance]** should be reallocated to Mega Jumbo in full — this is budget-neutral and immediately doubles trial yield based on store-week data. Invest Super Ultra refill budget in display and in-store placement instead.
- **Expected impact:** More than double new-buyer yield per promotional yen, no budget increase required.

---

### Key Insight 5: Half of All Shoppers Who Stop Buying Ariel Leave the Detergent Category Entirely

- **What we found:** ~50% of 1.78M lapsed buyers stopped buying all liquid detergent; only 23% switched to Attack.
- **Why it matters:** The category-exit group (~890,000/year) is Ariel's to win back — they are not locked into Attack.
- **What to do:** Post-renewal refill return rate improvements are already showing (Super Large +2.1pp, Ultra Jumbo +3.5pp) — maintain and extend.
- **Expected impact:** No specific re-engagement action is defined here — this is a follow-up workstream requiring a targeted reactivation campaign design.

## Risks to Watch

- **Attack may respond to an Ariel regular bottle price cut with a further reduction on its own bottle**, narrowing any recovered gap within weeks. Monitor weekly and be prepared to hold position rather than enter a price spiral; the goal is parity, not undercutting.
- **Price ceilings on trial events may conflict with short-term revenue targets.** Kept above the ceiling, a single Mega Jumbo promotional event appears to generate more revenue per unit — but it simultaneously destroys the repeat purchase that creates 5× the long-term value of a one-time sale.
- **This analysis covers loyalty cardholders only.** Non-member shoppers — estimated at 20–40% of total store volume — are not included. Actual scale of lapse and competitive switching across all buyers may differ from the numbers above.
- **"Left the category" may include e-commerce and club store purchases** not captured in the 9 retailers tracked. Some portion of the ~890,000 apparent category exits may have shifted channels rather than stopped buying detergent entirely. True category exit may be lower than the data suggests.
- **Post-renewal pricing improvements on refills are recent** (Super Large +2.1pp, Ultra Jumbo +3.5pp). These gains need to continue for 2–3 more quarters before they become durable trend signals rather than short-term bounces.

## How This Was Measured (Plain English)

- **Who is included:** Shoppers who hold loyalty cards at 9 national retailers and made at least one liquid laundry detergent purchase between January 2025 and January 2026. Each person is counted once per size category — not per transaction.
- **"First-time buyer"** means the first recorded Ariel purchase in the analysis window. Someone who bought Ariel before January 2025 and was still buying is not counted as a new buyer.
- **"Came back"** means the shopper made a second Ariel purchase within 6 months of their first. If no second purchase appeared within 6 months, they are counted as "stopped purchasing."
- **"Stopped purchasing / lapsed"** means no Ariel transaction was recorded for 6 or more months after the last purchase.
- **"Switched to Attack"** means the first liquid laundry purchase made after confirmed lapse was an Attack 抗菌EX product at a tracked retailer.
- **Average shelf prices** are calculated from actual scanned transaction amounts — not list prices or promotional price tags. They reflect what shoppers actually paid at checkout.
- **Price sensitivity figures** (e.g., "94 out of 100 observations") are based on weekly store-level data points across all 9 retailers. When we say "price cuts almost always bring in new buyers," we mean that across every store, every week where the price was reduced, 94% of those individual store-weeks showed an increase in first-time buyers for that size.
- **"Competitive capture rate"** (e.g., 95% of Mega Jumbo leavers going to Attack Mega Jumbo) is the share of confirmed lapsed Ariel buyers — whose next tracked detergent purchase was Attack — who bought the same pack size from Attack that they had been buying from Ariel.
